## Problem Statement

How can colleges identify gradual declines in student well-being early, without compromising student privacy, and connect students to appropriate support before the situation worsens?

---

## Problem in Our Words

**What needs to be solved?**
Students can slowly fall into a pattern of academic stress, poor sleep, and social isolation without anyone noticing. Many are unlikely to ask for help when they are struggling. Colleges need a way to spot declining well-being early, while respecting privacy, and connect students to the support they need.

**Why is it important now?**
Academic pressure and changing lifestyles affect students' sleep, stress, social connections, and overall well-being. Current support systems rely on students recognizing they are struggling and reaching out themselves, so early intervention often never happens.

---

## Who Is Affected?

**Primary users: students**
- All UG and PG students, especially first-years adjusting to campus life
- Hostel residents living away from home for the first time
- Final-years under placement and exam pressure
- "Quiet strugglers" who never walk into a counselling centre

**Secondary stakeholders**
- Counsellors and the wellness cell
- Mentors, faculty advisors, and wardens
- Parents and peers
- College administration

**Who benefits?**
- **Students:** earlier help, with control over their own data
- **Counsellors:** time goes to those who need it most
- **Colleges:** fewer crises, better retention, data-led wellness planning
- **Families and society:** fewer preventable tragedies

---

## Current Situation & Context

- Support depends on a counselling centre that students must approach on their own.
- Early signs (poor sleep, skipped classes, withdrawal) are scattered and never seen together.
- Mentors and wardens act on personal judgement, usually after grades drop.

**Key facts**
- About 75% of lifetime mental health conditions begin by age 24 (Kessler et al., 2005).
- India records 13,000+ student suicides a year (NCRB data).
- India's National Mental Health Survey (2015-16) found a 70-92% treatment gap across disorders.

**Today's workflow**
1. Stress builds quietly
2. Student stays silent
3. Others notice late
4. Ad-hoc referral
5. Help arrives late

---

## Key Challenges

**Difficulties and bottlenecks**
- Students rarely self-report because of stigma and fear of judgement
- Decline is gradual, with no single trigger moment
- Signals are fragmented across sleep, academics, and social life
- Anything that feels like surveillance is rejected

**Root causes**
- Reactive support model ("come to us when you need help")
- No personal baseline, so a bad week looks the same as a real decline
- No consent-based way to turn signals into support
- Few counsellors and no triage by risk or trend

**If left unsolved**
- Problems surface only at crisis: dropouts, failures, self-harm
- Recovery gets harder the longer students suffer
- Counsellors firefight instead of preventing
- Trust erodes, and legal and reputational risk grows

---

## Existing Solutions & Gaps

| Existing Approach | Where It Falls Short |
|---|---|
| Campus counselling centre | Walk-in based; relies on the student asking; stigma limits use |
| Faculty mentors and hostel wardens | Informal and subjective; notice late; no tools to triage |
| Helplines (Tele-MANAS 14416) and wellness apps | Self-initiated; disconnected from campus support; no trend detection |
| Annual surveys and awareness camps | One snapshot a year; aggregate only; cannot flag an individual |
| Academic early-warning (attendance, grades) | Fires only after academic damage; blind to sleep, stress, isolation |
